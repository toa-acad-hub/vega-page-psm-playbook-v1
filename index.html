Role Purpose, Success Metrics & Competency Levels

What the PSM role exists to do, how performance is measured, and what good looks like at every level.

1.1 What the PSM Role Is For

The People Service Manager (PSM) is TOA Global's frontline and people advocate — the most direct connection between team members (TMs) and the organisation. While clients direct the day-to-day work, the PSM owns the human side of the employment relationship: welfare, performance, engagement, escalation, and retention.

The role balances three responsibilities simultaneously:

  • People Leadership — welfare, coaching, development, and engagement
  • Operational Support — attendance, cases, onboarding, offboarding, compliance
  • Business Partnership — client coordination, departmental collaboration, data reporting, retention risk management

Section 02

Core Frameworks

The judgment and decision tools every PSM applies daily — from coaching conversations to escalation decisions.

2.1 The GROW Coaching Framework (30-Minute MR Structure)

GROW is the primary framework for all Meeting Rhythm (MR) coaching conversations. A standard monthly catch-up runs 30 minutes. Structure it as 25 minutes of conversation + 5 minutes of documentation. L1 PSMs use scripted conversation guides; L2+ adapt the structure to the TM's needs.

G
GOAL
5 min
What do you want to achieve from our conversation today? What does success look like for you this month?
Keep it focused — one primary goal per session
R
REALITY
5 min
Where are things right now? What's working well? What obstacles are you encountering with the client or in your role?
Listen actively. Let TM surface the issue — don't lead them
O
OPTIONS
8 min
What could you try? What has worked before? What support do you need from me or from the team?
Generate at least 3 options before evaluating any
W
WAY FORWARD
2 min
What will you commit to doing before our next catch-up? What does accountability look like?
Document the commitment in Polaris before ending the session
Pre-work: Review attendance trends, previous MR notes, client feedback, and the TM's pre-survey form before the session.

Post-work: Document in Polaris on the same day under Meeting Rhythm Coaching Activity. Advise TM to acknowledge meeting notes in Luna. Follow up on any takeaways, action items, or concerns raised during the MR through the appropriate forum.

GROW is an effective and high-impact coaching model that we expect every PSM to use as a basis for 1:1 check-in with your TMs. At a high level, you should be structuring each 1:1 for a 20-minute GROW conversation, with 10 minutes free for you to:

  • discuss other matters with the TM
  • finish with the TM & capture notes
  • prepare for your next 1:1
  • complete some administrative tasks

We provide a full day of coaching in this model, InsideOut Coaching, which is part of your mandatory training. If you have not been enrolled or would like to attend a refresher, please watch out for class schedule announcements or reach out to your Organizational Development Team.

2.2 The GRPI Model (TM Care Discovery & FGD Structure)

When a TM is enrolled into the TM Care Program, whether it's a 1:1 discussion or a facilitated group discussion (FGD), we use GRPI as the framework for determining root causes to issues raised.

GRPI works because most team member problems — disengagement, conflict, underperformance — trace back to one of four root causes: unclear goals, limited role clarity or unclear accountability, ineffective or missing processes, or interpersonal challenges with team relationships. The framework gives PSMs a structured way to diagnose which layer is actually the issue, rather than reacting to whatever surfaces first.

Important: we don't use GRPI for day-to-day standard 1:1s, please use GROW model.
G
Goals
Are TM and client expectations aligned? Does the TM understand what success looks like?
R
Roles
Is there clarity on who does what — between PSM, TM, CRM, and client?
P
Processes
Are the right workflows in place? Where are the friction points in daily operations?
I
Interpersonal
How are relationships? Is trust present? Are there unspoken tensions affecting performance?

2.4 Default to Transparency

This playbook operates on a principle of "default to open" — meaning information that helps people do their jobs better should be accessible, not gatekept. Applied to the PSM role:

  • Document everything in the right system, not just in your memory or a personal notebook
  • If you discover a process gap, flag it — don't work around it quietly
  • If you are handling a situation you've never faced before, say so and ask for input before acting
  • Your SM is not a gatekeeper — they are a resource. Use them proactively, not just when things go wrong
  • When you learn something, share it with the group. PSM quality improves when the team learns together

Each "play" is a structured approach for a common PSM scenario. They are not exhaustive SOPs — they are the judgment layer that sits above a checklist. For step-by-step instructions, follow the links in each play card.

3.1
Monthly Meeting Rhythm (MR) Coaching Session
▾
Overview
The foundational PSM activity. Every TM should have a structured 1:1 once per month. This is your primary mechanism for welfare, coaching, performance, and relationship. It is not a status update — it is a coaching conversation.
When to Use / Red Flags
  • TM has not had an MR in the current month
  • TM's pre-survey flags a concern topic
🚩 Red Flag
MR consistently rescheduled or avoided by TM — investigate why
Key Steps
  1. Send MR coaching invite to TM at least 24 hours before the session
  2. Complete pre-work: review Polaris attendance, previous MR notes, client feedback, Luna cases, check MR Pre-survey
  3. Open the session: confirm the primary topic from the pre-survey, set the tone (safe, focused)
  4. Run GROW framework: Goal (5 min) → Reality (8 min) → Options (8 min) → Way Forward (4 min)
  5. Close with clear commitments — who does what, by when
  6. Document in Polaris under 'Meeting Rhythm Coaching Activity' on the same day
  7. Flag any welfare concerns, attrition signals, or escalation needs to SM within 24 hours
Success Metrics
MR completion rate ≥90% monthly
Polaris documentation completed same-day
TM feedback: feels heard and supported
Zero overdue MR commitments in Polaris (Waive MR Requests via Polaris due to prolonged leaves or client request)
Acknowledged MRs in Luna 100%
Quarterly MR survey (automated)

3.2
TM Attrition Risk — Identification & Retention Intervention
▾
Overview
Attrition prevention is one of the highest-value activities a PSM can do. Early identification and a well-executed intervention can retain a TM who is on the verge of resigning. The cost of losing a TM — client disruption, replacement cost, team morale — far outweighs the effort of a proactive conversation.
When to Use / Red Flags
  • Attendance issues emerging (absences, tardiness patterns, TOA Hotline call-ins)
  • TM is withdrawn in MRs or stops engaging in team activities
  • TM has raised WFH / transfer / shift change requests or has shared major life events/changes (got married, got pregnant, upcoming maternity leave, etc.)
  • TM has raised communication/ engagement challenges affecting motivation
  • Client has flagged performance or behavioral concerns
  • TM is in 4–12 month tenure band — highest attrition risk window
  • TM in 1-2 years tenure band – reassessment on their commitment
  • TM nearing the end of their bond period (e.g., travel bond, WFH bond).
🚩 Red Flag
TM says 'I'm fine' consistently but body language, attendance, or performance data says otherwise
Key Steps
  1. Do not wait for the next scheduled MR — schedule an out-of-cycle check-in immediately
  2. Open the conversation with genuine curiosity, not an interrogation: 'I wanted to check in — how are things really going?'
  3. Apply GRPI discovery: surface whether the issue is role clarity, client relationship, workload, personal, or career
  4. Explore retention options (WFH eligibility, client adjustment, role match) before resignation is processed. Loop in SM and CRM— never make retention commitments without alignment
  5. If TM submits resignation: initiate Pre-Offboarding in Polaris immediately, schedule and conduct Resignation 1:1 within 48 hours
  6. Document all steps, commitments, and outcomes in Polaris
Success Metrics
Retention intervention success rate (TM Saved vs. TM Lost)
Attrition ≤2.25% MTD │ ≤27% YTD
Resignation 1:1 completed within 48 hours of submission
Exit feedback captured and shared with SM and OX

3.3
Performance Concern — Coaching to Escalation Pathway
▾
Overview
Performance concerns that are addressed early almost always resolve more cleanly than those left to fester. Your role is to coach first, document always, and escalate only when coaching has demonstrably not worked. Never surprise a TM — and never surprise PX or SM.
When to Use / Red Flags
  • Client has raised performance feedback informally or formally (via CRM)
  • TM's probationary metrics are below threshold
  • Failed MYPC and/or APR client evaluation
  • MR pattern shows repeated unresolved action items
  • Attendance trends correlate with performance decline
🚩 Red Flag
TM is defensive or dismissive when given feedback — this signals a deeper issue
Key Steps
  1. Gather data first: attendance records, client feedback, previous MR notes, Luna case history
  2. Have a direct but empathetic coaching conversation in the next MR (or out-of-cycle if urgent)
  3. Clearly name the concern using SBI framework: Situation → Behaviour → Impact
  4. Collaboratively set measurable improvement commitments with a specific timeline
  5. Document the coaching conversation in Polaris including commitments made
  6. If no improvement after 2–3 coaching cycles: loop in SM and align on next steps (PIP consideration)
  7. PIP initiation: coordinate with CRM and PX — do not serve without PX alignment and CRM go signal
  8. Weekly coaching sessions during PIP with PX present; document every session
Success Metrics
Coaching cycle completed before PIP initiation
PIP success rate (TM meets improvement criteria)
Probationary evaluations completed on time (3rd and 5th month)
SM satisfaction with documentation quality

3.4
New Hire Onboarding — Day 1 to First MR
▾
Overview
The first 30 days shape a TM's entire experience with TOA. A well-executed onboarding reduces early attrition, accelerates performance, and builds the trust that underpins every coaching conversation. This is also where PSM credibility is established.
When to Use / Red Flags
  • New hire has a confirmed start date in the onboarding tracker
  • Bench-to-client placement has been confirmed by Shift Manager
🚩 Red Flag
SM has not assigned PSM in tracker before start date — escalate immediately
Key Steps
  1. Pre-onboarding: Confirm your assignment in tracker, review client placement details, prepare Meet & Greet agenda
  2. Day 1: Meet TM in lobby. Conduct site tour. Verify system access, email, and tools with IT. Attend induction.
  3. Complete New Hire Meet & Greet: introduce your role, explain communication cadence, set expectations
  4. Explain probationary evaluation process (3rd / 5th month, criteria, what pass/fail means)
  5. Conduct First Rhythm Meeting after client induction (within first 2 weeks): check adjustment, address concerns, set MR schedule
  6. Update onboarding tracker in Polaris — same day, 100% accuracy
  7. Monitor 4–12 month tenure cohort proactively — highest-risk window for early attrition
Success Metrics
Day 1 onboarding tracker completion: 100%
First MR conducted within 14 days of start
New hire satisfaction (surveyed at 30 days)
Early attrition (0–6 months) trend

3.5
Disciplinary Process — NTE / NOD / Admin Hearing
▾
Overview
Disciplinary processes require procedural precision. PX and Labor Relations lead the formal process — your role is to prepare the TM, support the hearing, provide context, and ensure the TM is treated with dignity throughout. Never act unilaterally on formal HR matters.
When to Use / Red Flags
  • Attendance violations have reached the threshold for formal action
  • Client has filed a formal complaint about TM conduct
  • TM has committed a policy violation (confirmed, not alleged)
🚩 Red Flag
TM is panicking or confused about the process — your coaching role starts here
Key Steps
  1. Loop in SM immediately — do not proceed on any formal action without SM alignment
  2. LR/PX will prepare and issue the NTE — your role is to coach the TM on how to respond
  3. Explain the process to TM: what NTE means, what the administrative hearing is, what their rights are
  4. Attend the admin hearing to provide context on TM's performance, behaviour, and overall history — not to advocate for a specific outcome
  5. Contribute to NOD deliberation with LR/PX/CRM — provide a factual, objective account
  6. After NOD is issued, check in with TM: how are they feeling? What support do they need?
  7. Document all PSM coaching touchpoints in Polaris
Success Metrics
NTE/NOD compliance rate and procedural accuracy
Hearing conducted professionally — TM feedback
Zero procedural errors flagged by LR or PX
TM trust maintained post-process (follow-up MR NPS)

3.6
Daily Operations — Attendance, Inbox, Cases & Reporting
▾
Overview
The operational rhythm of a PSM day is structured but not rigid. These are the recurring activities that keep your TM portfolio running smoothly. Proactive monitoring prevents reactive firefighting.
When to Use / Red Flags
  • Start of every shift — daily non-negotiables
🚩 Red Flag
Case backlog in Titan, overdue MR actions in Polaris, attendance anomaly not followed up
Key Steps
  1. SOD (Start of Day): Check Luna for TM attendance and log-ins — flag anomalies immediately
  2. Monitor TOA Hotline for call-ins and ensure these are recorded in Polaris
  3. Inbox triage: prioritise urgent TM or CRM messages, respond within SLA
  4. Manage open Luna cases — aim for zero overdue cases in Titan (review daily)
  5. MOD (Mid of Day): Second attendance check, floorwalk if onsite, and daily compliance requirements (PX, COE, OX matters)
  6. Weekly: Review attendance dashboard (Power BI) for emerging patterns, prep SM huddle data
  7. Monthly: eNPS review, attrition report submission, compliance documentation (tardiness COA)
  8. Log all significant interactions and follow-ups in Polaris the same day they occur
Success Metrics
Luna attendance checked at SOD and MOD daily
Titan DCE tasks: zero overdue (TBC)
Tardiness ≤6% (≤3% Remote) threshold across portfolio
Weekly SM huddle data prepared and accurate
